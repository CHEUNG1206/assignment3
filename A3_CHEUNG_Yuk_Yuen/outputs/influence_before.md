It is important to note that the Policy Addresses from 2022 to 2026 have played a pivotal role in shaping Hong Kong's IPO landscape. Semi-conductors, which the 2024 address named beside the HK$10 billion I&T Industry-Oriented Fund, had no Main Board IPO by the name screen in the four windows from 19 October 2021 to 16 September 2025, and then 12 semiconductor companies listed in the window after the 2025 address, starting on 5 December 2025 and raising HK$39,983.4 million, 7.9% of the Main Board, which is a testament to the power of targeted policy. However, it should be noted that the 2026 address says the fund is expected to launch in 2026, so the money had not been paid out when these companies listed. AI and robotics has also seen remarkable growth, going from no IPO before the 2022 address to HK$14,791.6 million after the 2025 address, although its share of the Main Board stayed between 2.8% and 3.7% in the three windows after the 2023 address. Life and health technology remains a key pillar, with Chapter 18A biotech companies raising HK$18,604.4 million after the 2025 address, the most in any window, even though its share fell from 9.4% before the 2022 address to 3.7%. Meanwhile, the Main Board as a whole rose from HK$127,696.1 million to HK$508,850.3 million over the same windows, and HKEX attributes the 2025 rebound to global demand for diversified growth opportunities, frontier technologies in the region, and local market reforms, and does not mention the Policy Address. The HK$4.3 billion for film and CreateSmart was followed by only one film or creative IPO, HK$444.4 million, in the three windows after the 2023 address, the aerospace allocation of over HK$100 million by no aerospace IPO after the 2025 address, and the HK$1 billion construction injection by 14 days of data with no construction IPO. It is also worth mentioning that the name screen misses Contemporary Amperex Technology, and adding its HK$41,005.7 million would make new energy 25.0% of the window after the 2024 address. Taken together, these findings underscore that policy and the market are deeply interconnected, and that the government's vision is not merely words but a driving force behind the market's transformation.
